# Voice EC Map Web Market Research

Created: 2026-05-28

Research brief used: `VOICE_EC_MAP_MARKETING_CAMPAIGN.md`

## Executive Finding

Voice EC Map should not enter the market as an AI healthcare product, menopause treatment app, ADHD diagnostic tool, or generic coaching program.

The strongest launchable market position is:

> A human-reviewed, AI-assisted case organization service for midlife women and referral professionals who need to make ADHD-consistent, menopause-amplified, burnout/load, sleep/recovery, and body-state patterns easier to discuss before care or coaching.

The web market research supports this because:

- Menopause care is becoming a real funded category, with employer, telehealth, and benefits activity increasing.
- Adult ADHD awareness and diagnosis are rising, including in adult women.
- The visible competitor field is concentrated around treatment, coaching, education, virtual clinics, patient advocacy, and symptom apps.
- There is still whitespace between "I need medical treatment" and "I need generic coaching": structured pre-session signal, differential-safe pattern mapping, and coach/case packets.
- AI-agent claims are risky unless framed as bounded operations with human review.

Bottom line:

> Launch as a premium, scoped, non-diagnostic case clarity service first. Use AI agents to run the firm and improve throughput, not as the headline clinical authority.

## Best Market Wedge

The best wedge is not "menopause care" by itself. That market already has several stronger clinical players.

The best wedge is:

> Midlife cognition ambiguity for women who are still functioning but losing private capacity.

The specific buying question:

> "I need to organize what is happening before I talk to a coach, clinician, PMHNP, OB-GYN, therapist, or care team."

This fits Voice EC Map because the product already has:

- a structured 64-item spine
- eight voice-sized sections
- client report
- coach packet
- ADHD-equal and menopause-equal lens
- deterministic scoring
- non-diagnostic scope
- safety separation
- claim guardrails

## Market Context

### Menopause And Midlife Care

The menopause market has moved from an under-served niche into an active healthcare and benefits category.

Signals found online:

- PwC frames menopause as a large healthcare and workplace market, estimating a current multi-billion-dollar market and a larger 2030 opportunity.
- PwC also highlights low rates of care-seeking despite symptom burden, which supports a case-preparation wedge.
- Menopause-focused companies such as Midi Health, Evernow, Alloy, Elektra Health, Gennev, Winona, Stella, and others now make the category visible.
- Several competitors focus on clinical care, hormone therapy, prescriptions, education, or employer benefits.

Voice EC Map implication:

> Do not compete head-on with virtual menopause clinics. Position as the preparation layer that helps clients enter those conversations with a cleaner functional story.

### Adult ADHD

CDC data and public ADHD care options indicate a large adult ADHD market.

Signals found online:

- CDC reported that roughly 15.5 million U.S. adults had a current ADHD diagnosis in 2023, with a large share using telehealth for ADHD care.
- ADHD Online and similar services sell online diagnostic/evaluation workflows.
- ADHD coaching apps such as Shimmer and Inflow sell support, skills, coaching, or app-based behavior change.
- Online customer language around women, ADHD, perimenopause, brain fog, and capacity loss is strong.

Voice EC Map implication:

> Do not sell diagnosis. Sell structured pattern clarification before evaluation, coaching, or care planning.

### AI Agents In Healthcare And Services

AI-agent adoption is visible, but trust is uneven.

Signals found online:

- McKinsey's 2025 AI research reports broad AI adoption but uneven scaling and governance.
- Healthcare AI companies increasingly sell scribes, intake support, documentation, care navigation, and workflow automation.
- FTC enforcement around AI claims makes unsupported "AI-powered" promises a launch risk.

Voice EC Map implication:

> "Run by AI agents" can be an operating model. It should not be the primary trust claim. The public message should be "AI-assisted, human-reviewed, bounded workflow."

Relevant market pools:

The broad market is large enough to support a niche service. Voice EC Map does not need to own a large share of any category to work as a solopreneur firm.

### SAM: Serviceable Niche

The serviceable niche is narrower:

> U.S.-based midlife women, coaches, care managers, and small practices dealing with ADHD/menopause/cognition ambiguity where the buyer wants structured preparation, not diagnosis.

Likely best initial subsegments:

1. Women 38-58 with high functional load and capacity decline.

3. Menopause coaches and consultants who need better cognition/capacity intake.
4. PMHNP-adjacent, OT-adjacent, and care-navigation professionals who want cleaner pre-session context.
5. Small women's health practices that do not yet have a structured intake layer for cognition/capacity questions.

## White Space

The white space is a productized consulting category:

> Midlife capacity case mapping.

This is not a mainstream category yet, but the underlying demand is visible across menopause care, ADHD support, coaching, patient advocacy, and AI intake.

### What Existing Categories Miss

| Category | What It Solves | What It Misses |
|---|---|---|
| Menopause clinic | treatment, symptoms, prescriptions, hormone care | lifelong ADHD-consistent pattern, masking cost, appointment prep packet |
| ADHD evaluation | diagnosis/evaluation | menopause amplification and body-state load |
| ADHD coaching | implementation/accountability | structured differential-safe case packet |
| Therapy | emotional processing and mental health care | functional capacity map for cross-provider conversation |
| Patient advocacy | navigation and appointment support | specialized ADHD/menopause/capacity framework |
| Generic AI chatbot | flexible reflection | evidence boundary, safety separation, deterministic scoring |

### Voice EC Map Differentiators

1. ADHD and menopause remain co-equal.
2. Product is explicitly non-diagnostic.
3. Output is not just self-insight; it becomes a coach/case packet.
4. AI agents are operational, not clinical authorities.
5. Safety and scope are separated from public marketing and report generation.
6. Service can launch before a full SaaS build.

## Launch Research Questions

These should be answered through interviews and sales calls, not web research alone.

### Direct Client

1. What phrase do they use first: brain fog, ADHD, menopause, burnout, executive function, capacity, or overwhelm?
2. Do they want a report for themselves, or a packet to bring to someone else?
3. What would make $500 feel reasonable?
4. Who do they trust to review the packet?
5. Do they prefer voice intake or written intake?
6. What privacy language lowers friction?
7. What would make them say "this is too clinical" or "this is too lightweight"?

### Referral Partner

1. What part of intake currently wastes first-session time?
2. Would they refer a client to a separate case-mapping service?
3. Would they rather receive a client-facing report, a coach packet, or both?
4. What would trigger scope concerns?
5. What information would they not want the packet to include?
6. What price would be acceptable for clients?
7. Would they pay for a practice pilot?

## Research-Backed Positioning Update

Replace:

> A solopreneur case management consulting firm run by AI agents.

With:

> A human-reviewed, AI-assisted case clarity service for midlife cognition and capacity questions.

Then explain agents operationally:

> AI agents help prepare content, organize intake, draft structured packets, check claims, track follow-up, and monitor campaign performance. The service remains bounded, non-diagnostic, and human-reviewed.

Reason:

- "Run by AI agents" creates trust and compliance friction if it leads.
- "Human-reviewed, AI-assisted" fits the actual product boundary.
- The buyer cares more about the report, packet, and clarity than the internal automation.

## Final Market Verdict

Voice EC Map has a credible launch wedge if it stays narrow.

The best first market is not everyone with menopause symptoms, everyone with ADHD, or every care-management buyer. It is:

> Midlife women and the professionals who support them when the functional story is too complex for a generic symptom checklist and too early or too ambiguous for a single clinical label.

The service should sell:

- clarity before appointment
- pattern before label
- coach packet before first session
- bounded AI before autonomous agent claims
- human review before automation hype

That is the defensible path to first revenue.
